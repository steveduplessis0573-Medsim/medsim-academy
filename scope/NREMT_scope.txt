## EMT AUTHORIZATIONS (BLS MODE) — DO NOT FLAG THESE AS VIOLATIONS

OXYGEN THERAPY: All providers. Nasal cannula 1-6 LPM; non-rebreather mask 10-15 LPM; BVM with reservoir. Titrate to SpO2 ≥ 94% in most patients; COPD — titrate to 88-92%.

AIRWAY ADJUNCTS: OPA (unconscious patients without gag reflex); NPA (semi-conscious or gag reflex present). Suction — rigid (Yankauer) or flexible catheter. All EMT authorized.

BAG-VALVE-MASK (BVM) VENTILATION: EMT authorized. 1 breath every 5-6 seconds (adult); 1 breath every 3-5 seconds (pediatric). Use 2-person technique when available.

ASPIRIN 324-325 mg PO: EMT authorized. Suspected ACS / chest pain of cardiac origin. Contraindicated: true aspirin allergy, active GI bleed, patient already took full dose.

NITROGLYCERIN 0.4 mg SL: EMT authorized (patient's own prescription or protocol). Suspected ACS / angina. Max 3 doses q5 min. Contraindicated: SBP < 100, PDE5 inhibitor use within 48-72 hours, suspected inferior/RV MI with hypotension.

EPINEPHRINE AUTO-INJECTOR (1:1,000): EMT authorized. Anaphylaxis/severe allergic reaction. Adult (≥ 30 kg): 0.3 mg IM auto-injector. Pediatric (< 30 kg): 0.15 mg IM auto-injector. May repeat once in 5-15 min if no improvement. Do NOT flag auto-injector use as a scope violation for EMTs.

ALBUTEROL (MDI or SVN): EMT authorized — assist patient with their own inhaler OR administer per protocol. Bronchospasm / reactive airway disease / asthma. 2.5 mg via SVN or 2 puffs MDI. Contraindicated: HR > 150 with symptoms (use clinical judgment).

ORAL GLUCOSE GEL: EMT authorized. Hypoglycemia with confirmed low BGL in a patient who is conscious and able to protect their airway. Do NOT administer to unconscious or seizing patients.

NALOXONE (Narcan): EMT authorized. 2 mg intranasal (IN) or 0.4 mg IM. Suspected opioid overdose with respiratory depression. May repeat in 2-4 min. Note: short duration — patient may re-sedate before hospital arrival.

ACTIVATED CHARCOAL 1 g/kg PO (max 50 g): EMT authorized where protocols permit. Ingested poisoning within 1 hour, patient alert and able to protect airway. Contraindicated: altered mental status, caustic/corrosive ingestion, hydrocarbon ingestion.

CPAP: EMT authorized per NSES. Initiate at 5 cm H2O for respiratory distress / suspected pulmonary edema. Contraindicated: vomiting, AMS, BP < 90, facial trauma preventing seal.

AED / SEMI-AUTOMATIC DEFIBRILLATION: EMT authorized. Shockable rhythms (VF/pulseless VT). Follow AED prompts. Resume CPR immediately after shock — do not delay for rhythm check.

CPR: All providers. 30:2 ratio (adult/pediatric with single rescuer); continuous compressions with advanced airway. Rate 100-120/min, depth 2-2.4 inches adult, 2 inches pediatric.

HEMORRHAGE CONTROL: Tourniquet application, wound packing, direct pressure. EMT authorized for all bleeding control including junctional wounds.

SPINAL MOTION RESTRICTION: EMT authorized. Apply based on mechanism and clinical findings. NREMT expects selective spinal immobilization — not automatic C-collar on every trauma.

SPLINTING: EMT authorized. Traction splint for mid-shaft femur fractures. Rigid/soft splints for extremity injuries. Assess PMS before and after.

BLOOD GLUCOSE MONITORING: EMT authorized. Check BGL in any patient with AMS, suspected diabetic emergency, or altered behavior.

PULSE OXIMETRY / CAPNOGRAPHY: EMT monitoring. SpO2 continuous during transport. ETCO2 monitoring with advanced airway.

12-LEAD ECG ACQUISITION: EMT authorized per current NSES (acquisition and transmission only). EMTs do NOT interpret — forward to medical direction or receiving facility.

OBSTETRIC / DELIVERY: EMT authorized. Normal delivery, nuchal cord management, meconium suctioning, newborn resuscitation basics.


## PARAMEDIC AUTHORIZATIONS (ALS MODE) — DO NOT FLAG AS VIOLATIONS

VASCULAR ACCESS: IV (peripheral — any gauge), IO (tibial, humeral, sternal per device). PICC/central line access permitted in life-threatening situations if no other access available.

ENDOTRACHEAL INTUBATION (ETT): Paramedic authorized. Direct laryngoscopy or video laryngoscopy. Confirm with waveform capnography — mandatory. 2 attempts maximum before moving to surgical airway.

SUPRAGLOTTIC AIRWAY (SGA / LMA / King LT): Paramedic and EMT authorized as rescue airway.

NEEDLE CRICOTHYROTOMY / SURGICAL CRICOTHYROTOMY: Paramedic authorized. Failed airway — cannot intubate, cannot oxygenate (CICO situation).

NEEDLE THORACOSTOMY: Paramedic authorized. Tension pneumothorax — severe respiratory distress + absent unilateral breath sounds + hemodynamic compromise.

SYNCHRONIZED CARDIOVERSION: Paramedic authorized. Unstable tachydysrhythmia with pulse. 100-200 J biphasic initial.

MANUAL DEFIBRILLATION: Paramedic authorized. VF / pulseless VT. 200 J biphasic (or manufacturer recommendation) — resume CPR immediately.

TRANSCUTANEOUS PACING: Paramedic authorized. Symptomatic bradycardia unresponsive to atropine.

12-LEAD ECG ACQUISITION AND INTERPRETATION: Paramedic authorized. Identify STEMI criteria and activate cath lab (Code STEMI) if present. Failure to identify and notify = CRITICAL VIOLATION.

ADENOSINE: Paramedic. SVT (regular narrow-complex). 6 mg rapid IVP + 20 ml NS flush; 12 mg if no response. Contraindicated: 2nd/3rd degree AV block, WPW with irregular rhythm.

ALBUTEROL (extended): Paramedic may continue beyond initial 2 doses; use for hyperkalemia (5 mg SVN). Do not administer simultaneously with epinephrine.

AMIODARONE: Paramedic. VF/pulseless VT arrest: 300 mg IVP, repeat 150 mg. Stable VT: 150 mg over 10 min. Contraindicated: cardiogenic shock, 2nd/3rd degree AV block.

ATROPINE (cardiac): Paramedic. Symptomatic bradycardia < 60 BPM. 0.5-1.0 mg IVP q3-5 min, max 3 mg. Ineffective in denervated (transplanted) hearts.

ATROPINE (toxicology): Paramedic. Organophosphate/nerve agent poisoning. Titrate to secretion reduction, no maximum dose in true toxidrome.

BENZODIAZEPINES (Midazolam/Diazepam/Lorazepam): Paramedic. Seizures, sedation, pre-cardioversion, behavioral emergency. Midazolam: 2.5-5 mg IV/IM/IN. Diazepam: 5-10 mg IV. Contraindicated: SBP < 90, respiratory insufficiency.

CALCIUM CHLORIDE: Paramedic. Hyperkalemia with ECG changes, CCB toxicity, hypocalcemia. 1 g IV/IO. Contraindicated: digoxin toxicity.

DEXTROSE (D10W or D50W): Paramedic (IV/IO route). Confirmed hypoglycemia with AMS. D10W preferred. Contraindicated: stroke/suspected intracranial event with BGL > 60.

DIPHENHYDRAMINE (Benadryl): Paramedic. Allergic reactions, acute dystonic reactions. 25-50 mg IV/IM.

DOPAMINE INFUSION: Paramedic. Cardiogenic shock, symptomatic hypotension. 2-20 mcg/kg/min IV infusion.

EPINEPHRINE IV (1:10,000): Paramedic. Cardiac arrest all rhythms: 1 mg IV/IO q3-5 min. Severe anaphylaxis/imminent arrest: 0.1-0.5 mg IV/IO.

EPINEPHRINE INFUSION (push-dose / drip): Paramedic. Shock not responding to fluids, post-ROSC hypotension, refractory bradycardia. Push-dose: 10-20 mcg IV boluses. Drip: 2-10 mcg/min.

FENTANYL: Paramedic. Pain management, procedural sedation. 1-2 mcg/kg IV/IO/IM/IN. Caution: respiratory depression, hypotension, elderly/frail patients.

GLUCAGON: Paramedic. Hypoglycemia without IV access: 1 mg IM. Beta-blocker/CCB toxicity: 3 mg IV slow push.

KETAMINE: Paramedic. Sub-dissociative pain (0.1-0.3 mg/kg IV), procedural sedation (1-2 mg/kg IV), excited delirium/behavioral emergency (4-5 mg/kg IM). Contraindicated: severe HTN, known schizophrenia, age < 3 months.

LIDOCAINE: Paramedic. Ventricular dysrhythmias, IO pain (40 mg IO before infusion). 1-1.5 mg/kg IVP for dysrhythmia.

MAGNESIUM SULFATE: Paramedic. Torsades de Pointes: 2 g IV over 2 min (arrest) or 10 min (stable). Severe asthma refractory to bronchodilators: 2 g IV over 20 min. Eclampsia: 4-6 g IV over 15-20 min.

MORPHINE SULFATE: Paramedic. Pain, pulmonary edema (with SBP > 100). 2-4 mg IV q5-10 min. Contraindicated: SBP < 90, severe respiratory depression.

NITROGLYCERIN IV/SL (ALS expanded): Paramedic. SL same as EMT. IV drip for refractory pulmonary edema or hypertensive emergency: 10-20 mcg/min titrate.

ONDANSETRON (Zofran): Paramedic (all routes including IV/IM). 4 mg IV/IM/ODT. Nausea/vomiting, post-opioid nausea. Contraindicated: known prolonged QT.

SODIUM BICARBONATE: Paramedic. TCA overdose, suspected acidosis in arrest, hyperkalemia with cardiac involvement. 1 mEq/kg IV/IO.

VASOPRESSIN: Paramedic. Cardiac arrest (alternative to epinephrine, per some protocols). 40 units IVP once.

## VIOLATION SEVERITY — APPLY IN ALL NREMT MODE DEBRIEFS

CRITICAL VIOLATION: The provider performed an action explicitly outside their certification level per NSES, administered a medication with a hard contraindication present, or failed to perform a mandatory skill expected of a competent provider at that level. Cite as: "per National EMS Education Standards [EMT/Paramedic] competencies." These reduce the score and may result in FAIL.

CLINICAL CONCERN (NOT a CRITICAL VIOLATION): The provider made a within-scope decision that carries clinical risk — poor timing, suboptimal sequencing, or questionable judgment — but did not cross a scope boundary or violate a hard contraindication. Label as "CLINICAL CONCERN." Do NOT penalize equally to true violations. Coach, do not cite.

SCOPE BOUNDARY NOTE: In NREMT mode, do not cite agency-specific protocols, page numbers, or PWC-specific drug thresholds. Evaluate solely against NSES competencies and standard national EMS practice.
